The Kit4Kids Air Record Fine particulate matter · Chiang Mai — not recording

Three rooms, three monitors, one figure worth watching

A continuously updated record of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) at the Kit4Kids stations, set from the readings the stations publish for themselves.3

Stations DBK-001, DBK-002 and DBK-010 · typeset live as the readings arrive

Three small monitors sit in three rooms in Chiang Mai. Each measures the mass of fine particles floating in the air around it — smoke, soot, dust small enough to slip past the nose and settle in a lung — and publishes that mass, in micrograms per cubic metre, as often as it can. What follows is that publication, set as it arrives.

Nothing here is averaged, smoothed or filled in. Where a station is silent, the space where its figure would go is left empty and said so;2 a dash in this record is never a zero, and a zero is never a stand-in for a monitor that has stopped speaking.

Notes and references

  1. Breakpoints are those of the United States Environmental Protection Agency for PM2.5: 0–12.0 good; 12.1–35.4 moderate; 35.5–55.4 unhealthy for sensitive groups; 55.5–150.4 unhealthy; 150.5–250.4 very unhealthy; 250.5 and above hazardous, all in µg/m³. See U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Technical Assistance Document for the Reporting of Daily Air Quality — the Air Quality Index (AQI), Office of Air Quality Planning and Standards (revised 2024), breakpoint table for PM2.5. Those breakpoints are defined for a 24-hour average; the figures printed here are single instantaneous readings placed on the same scale. That is a simplification, and it is stated rather than hidden: a brief spike reads worse here than the EPA method would score a whole day.
  2. Readings are published without the retained flag, so a station that is alive but has not spoken since this page opened is printed as awaiting rather than carrying a figure. A figure older than the station’s declared expiry is marked expired and is not treated as current. Two of the three stations are ordinarily silent; that is the normal state of this record, not a fault in the page.
  3. Each station announces itself over MQTT discovery, and the announcement names every one of them PM2.5. The station identity printed in this record is therefore parsed from the state topic (dbk/DBK-002/pm2_5), never from the announced name, which cannot tell one room from another.
  4. This page subscribes only to concrete topics and to three bounded discovery patterns of its own. It never subscribes to a wildcard that arrives in a payload, and never to the whole tree.
